Good to know

  • JPEG photos are normalised at high quality so phone orientation is respected.
  • PNG images keep their transparency; transparent areas use the PDF background.
  • Animated GIFs become their first frame.
  • SVG files are rendered by the browser at their natural size.
  • Every page is an image; this is not a scan-to-text tool.
  • Cancel applies while images are being prepared. Once PDF assembly starts, it finishes rather than pretending to stop.

Memory limits

Large photo collections consume real memory. If a batch fails, reduce the resolution, use fewer images, or split it into two PDFs. A browser tab has no swap file of its own.

Practical guide

Make a PDF that looks right on paper and on screen

Image → PDF is for photos, scans, receipts, screenshots and artwork that should become one document. The controls are deliberately small: order, rotation, page size, fit and quality. Here is how to choose them.

Common jobs and settings

  • Phone photos of documents: choose A4, portrait, fit inside, 10–20 mm margin. The browser applies EXIF orientation first, so pages are not sideways.
  • Receipts or long screenshots: choose Original image size to avoid trimming detail, then reduce resolution only if the PDF is too large to share.
  • Portfolio or lookbook: choose US Letter or A4, landscape, and Fill the page only when cropping the edges is acceptable. Fit inside preserves the whole image.
  • Scanned forms: keep the original resolution and add a modest margin. Every page is an image, so text will not be selectable or searchable.

Page size, fit and quality

A4210 × 297 mm. The usual choice for printing outside North America.
US Letter8.5 × 11 in. The usual choice for printing in the US and Canada.
OriginalThe page follows each image's own dimensions. Useful for screenshots and irregular scans.
Fit insideShows the entire image with margins; nothing is cropped.
Fill pageFills the page and crops overflow. Good for edge-to-edge photos, wrong for documents with important borders.
Resolution capDownscales the longest side before embedding. 3200 px is a good print-oriented cap; 1200 px is a lighter sharing copy.

Frequently asked questions

Will the PDF text be selectable?

No. Each page is an embedded image, so text inside a photo stays part of the picture. This tool performs no OCR and does not claim to create searchable PDFs.

Are my images uploaded to build the PDF?

No. Images are decoded by your browser and assembled with pdf-lib inside a local Web Worker. Previews use temporary object URLs that are revoked when you remove an image or leave the page. Nothing is written to a server.

Why did my transparent PNG change colour?

Transparent areas take on the PDF page background, which is white. If you need a different backdrop, flatten the image onto that colour in an image editor before adding it here.

Can I add more images after generating the PDF?

Yes. The queue stays in the tab after generation. Add or remove images, change the order, and generate again. Use Start over to clear the queue completely.

What happens to animated GIFs and SVGs?

GIFs become a single still frame. SVGs are rendered by the browser at their natural size. Animated AVIF/WebP sources also lose their animation.

Can I cancel a large job?

Cancel works while images are being read or prepared. Once PDF assembly begins, the worker finishes the document rather than pretending to stop mid-write. For very large batches, use a lower resolution cap or split the job.

Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to try
An image is rejectedThe browser could not decode it. Re-save it as PNG or JPG in an image editor, then add it again.
Pages are rotated incorrectlyUse the rotate control before generating. EXIF orientation is applied automatically, but an image with missing or wrong metadata may still need a manual 90° turn.
The PDF is hugeSet a resolution cap (3200/2200/1200 px) or use JPG-friendly source photos. Every page embeds image data, so resolution is the main size driver.
Edges are cut offSwitch from Fill the page to Fit inside, or reduce the margin.
The tab runs out of memoryRemove some images, lower the resolution cap, or create two smaller PDFs. A browser tab has finite memory and no swap file of its own.
